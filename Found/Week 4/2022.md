## Q10 — Interpreting a statement about fooling people

**Question.** Let $F(x,y,t)$ mean that person $x$ can fool person $y$ at time $t$. Which choice best expresses this formula?

$$
\forall x\,\exists y\,\exists t\;\neg F(x,y,t)
$$

| Choice | Meaning, paraphrased |
|---|---|
| A | Every individual can fool at least one person at some time. |
| B | Nobody can fool everybody at every time. |
| C | Everyone fails to fool some individual at all times. |
| D | Nobody can fool anybody at any time. |

**Answer: B.** For each person, there is at least one person and at least one time at which the attempt to fool that person fails.

### Read the quantifiers in order

Start with any person $x$. The formula requires a person $y$ and a time $t$ for which $\neg F(x,y,t)$ holds. The witnesses $y$ and $t$ may depend on $x$: different people may fail to fool different people at different times.

Only one such failure is required for each $x$. The formula does not say that every attempt fails, that the same person resists everybody, or that a particular person resists deception at every time.

### Derive the English statement

“Someone can fool everybody at every time” is written as

$$
\exists x\,\forall y\,\forall t\;F(x,y,t).
$$

Negating that statement gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\neg\exists x\,\forall y\,\forall t\;F(x,y,t)
&\equiv \forall x\,\neg\forall y\,\forall t\;F(x,y,t)\\
&\equiv \forall x\,\exists y\,\neg\forall t\;F(x,y,t)\\
&\equiv \forall x\,\exists y\,\exists t\;\neg F(x,y,t).
\end{aligned}
$$

This is exactly the supplied formula. Therefore nobody has universal success over all people and all times, which is choice B.

### Check the other choices

**A changes failure into success.** Its positive claim would use $F(x,y,t)$ rather than $\neg F(x,y,t)$. Knowing that everyone fails at least once does not establish that everyone succeeds at least once.

**C uses awkward scope in the original question.** If it means that each person has a particular individual whom they can never fool, it would require

$$
\forall x\,\exists y\,\forall t\;\neg F(x,y,t).
$$

That is stronger: a single failure at one time does not imply failure at every time. If the English “not ... all times” is instead read with negation over the universal time claim, it can express the supplied formula. This ambiguity makes B the clearest choice among the printed options.

**D forbids every success.** It would require $\forall x\,\forall y\,\forall t\;\neg F(x,y,t)$. The supplied formula permits many successful attempts as long as each person has at least one failure.

### A small example

Suppose Arun fools Bela in the morning but fails to fool her in the evening. Bela fools Arun in the evening but fails to fool him in the morning. Each person has a failure, so neither fools everybody at every time. Successful attempts still occur, showing why D is too strong.

**Recall check:** A negation pushed through a quantifier exchanges $\forall$ and $\exists$. Track both the quantifier and the position of the negation before choosing an English translation.
